I See You, Aizawa-san!

I See You, Aizawa-san! is a Horror, Mystery, Romance manga written and illustrated by Oodoro Dorothy. The series follows Michi Kurotori, a girl who can see ghosts and, ever since a traumatic moment of her childhood, has tried to make sure that the ghosts don't find this out. After coming back to class after the holidays, she learns that one of her classmates (and famous idol), Honami Aizawa, has died, and her ghost is now haunting the classroom. However, she is different from the other ghosts, she is more normal and human-like. Various comedic antics ensue with Michi trying not to let Aizawa know she can see her, yet not being able to take her eyes off her, all the while a mystery grows in the background surrounding Aizawa’s death and her old idol group.

I love how this series balances comedy and horror, at first it may not seem like a combination that would work, but when you realize that the makings of good horror and good comedy are quite similar, it makes a lot more sense. Horror and comedy are all about buildup and release. This series is mostly a comedy, but every now and then, you'll turn the page to something actually quite scary. The humour in this series is actually quite effective, it leans into absurdity and randomness. I think the funniest characters are Sayaka and Seta, Sayaka seems cool and prince-like on the outside but is actually an idiot, she can also see ghosts, but she just acts like they're normal instead of ignoring them or treating them like ghosts, the series never makes it clear whether she actually thinks that nothing's wrong or if this is just her method to avoid harm. Seta barely shows up, but whenever he does, he's always falls into the archetype of the crazy person who's saying crazy things that no one else believes (like Joyce in Stranger Things season 1). What makes him different from this trope is that he is coincidentally right about most of the things he’s saying, he is still crazy and delusional, but his delusions just so happen to be right, he has no idea about this though. The horror is also very good, sometimes the chapter will be really funny throughout, only to start subtly building to something, then hit you with a crazy page turn. The art can go from goofy and funny to very scary with the turn of a page, and it almost leans into body horror with how some of the ghosts look.

The overarching mysteries of the series are also heavily tied to both the comedy and horror, a lot of the horror scenes bring either more questions or a few answers, and a lot of the comedy scenes will have offhanded statements that do the same. This is a series you really have to pay attention too in order to pick up on everything. A clear example of this Michi’s memory loss, it is never really made clear until the festival arc, but you can pick up on it from her first meeting with Kasumi, where she says she hasn’t talked to anyone all year and has to psych herself up to talk to her, only for Kasumi to immediately act familiar with her, then in a later chapter we get a flashback from Kasumi’s perspective and it reveals that they have talked before the first meeting we saw, which goes against what Michi said, all of that is shown in a panel or two and it doesn’t pay close attention to these scenes. I like this method of storytelling and mystery as it makes you really sink your teeth into the series and gets you excited for when the next chapter releases.

I do have more I could talk about with this series but I need to keep this short. I love this series and I highly recommend it, especially if you like mysteries. Its also good to mention that the first volume of the official English translation is releasing early 2027 so keep an eye out for that.

Rating: 9/10
